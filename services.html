After-sales

The after-sales scope is part of the supply.

A crane that arrives without commissioning, or a tow tractor without operator training, is an unfinished delivery. These services exist so the equipment is actually in service when we close the file.

01

Installation, commissioning and calibration

Site installation, commissioning to the manufacturer's specification and instrument calibration, with a signed handover record. Where the site requires civil or electrical preparation, that scope is identified before delivery rather than discovered during it.

Discuss this scope
02

Maintenance and operational training

Operator and maintenance training delivered on your site, on your equipment. The objective is a team that can run the unit safely and carry out first-line maintenance without waiting for a service visit.

Discuss this scope
03

Repair and maintenance

Fault diagnosis, repair and scheduled maintenance. We source parts through the same principal relationships the equipment came from, so a repair does not depend on an unofficial substitute.

Discuss this scope
04

Indexing, demonstration and procurement support

A new product must be indexed with the civilian departments and the Ministry of Defence before it can be introduced to a user unit. We handle indexing, arrange the demonstration and support the procurement process through to contract.

Discuss this scope
05

Parts and long-term support

A continuing spares path for the equipment we supply, including discontinued models that remain in service. Send the part number, or a photograph of the failed item, and we will identify the source.

Discuss this scope

Why it matters

Discontinued does not mean abandoned.

Equipment bought in 1995 is often still in service. A supplier who disappears when the model is superseded leaves the user with a machine nobody will support.

We continue to support discontinued models for parts and service, because that is what the equipment in the field requires.

01

Commissioning records signed at handover.

The record names the configuration and the person who accepted it.

02

Training on site, on your equipment.

Operators and maintainers learn the unit they will actually run.

03

Parts through the original principal.

The part number and the traceability document come from the manufacturer, not a substitute.

04

Discontinued models supported.

Old units remain in the spares path for as long as they are in service.
